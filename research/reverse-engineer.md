# reverse-engineer

**Purpose first.** Ask or infer which: `ours` (write it down) · `reference` (what is worth taking) · `compare` (several side by side) · `one idea` (understand one thing). The purpose decides what is kept.

1. Intake the thing (`read-input` step 1): whose, shows `exists`.
2. **What is there**: list places, parts, flows, rules, as they appear; say how sure you are per item (seen · inferred · guessed).
3. **How it behaves**: states, edge cases, errors, as observed.
4. **Why, guessed**: for each area, the most likely reason, marked `[assumed]`. Batch the questions per area; never one at a time.
5. By purpose:
   - ours → checkpoint per area; the librarian writes cards marked `as found` until the user confirms the area.
   - reference → `sandbox/research/<name>.md`: what is worth taking, each with how well it fits our product and what it would conflict with; nothing enters the KB. The product owner translates to our terms and pushes back; a picked piece is parked or adopted through `change-impact`, linked to its inspiration.
   - compare → a table: what all have, what only some have, what we lack; then as reference.
   - one idea → the idea, its mechanism, its why; to checkpoint as a fact or an idea.
6. Depth: stop at the level the user is working at; say what was not read.
